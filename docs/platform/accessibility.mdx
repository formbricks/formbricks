Formbricks is used to ask people questions. A survey that some people cannot answer is a survey that returns
incomplete data, so accessibility is not a compliance exercise for us — it is part of the product working
correctly.

This page describes the accessibility of the **public survey UI**: the interface a respondent sees when they
open a link survey or are shown a website or in-app survey. It covers what already works, what does not, and
how to reach us.

<Note>
  This page covers the respondent-facing survey UI only. The Formbricks application that survey creators use
  to build surveys, and the self-hosting and administration interfaces, have not been assessed to the same
  standard and are not described here.
</Note>

---

## Standards We Measure Against

Formbricks assesses the public survey UI against two standards:

| Standard | Level assessed |
| --- | --- |
| Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.0 | Level A and Level AA |
| Revised Section 508 standards (published 18 January 2017, corrected 22 January 2018) | Chapters 4, 5, 6 and 7 |

Our assessment is published as a Voluntary Product Accessibility Template (VPAT) using the **VPAT 2.5Rev,
Revised Section 508 Edition** format. The most recent report was completed on **9 August 2026**.

To request the full report, ask in
[GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/formbricks/formbricks/discussions).

---

## Conformance Status

The public survey UI **partially conforms** to WCAG 2.0 Level AA. Partial conformance means that most of the
standard is met, but some content does not fully conform.

The known gaps are listed under [Known Limitations](#known-limitations) below. We publish them rather than
summarising them away, because a procurement team or an individual respondent needs to know what will not work
before they rely on it, not after.

## Accessibility Features

Everything in this table was assessed as supported in the most recent report.

| Area | What the survey UI does | WCAG 2.0 criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keyboard operation | Every question type, navigation button, and control can be operated with a keyboard alone. Keyboard focus is never trapped in a component — it can always be moved away using standard keys. | 2.1.1, 2.1.2 |
| Visible focus | The element that currently has keyboard focus is shown with a visible focus indicator. | 2.4.7 |
| Focus order | Focus moves through the survey in an order that preserves meaning and operability. | 2.4.3 |
| Text alternatives | Non-text content is given a text alternative. | 1.1.1 |
| Contrast | Text and images of text meet the minimum contrast ratio. | 1.4.3 |
| Resizing text | Text can be resized up to 200% without assistive technology, and without loss of content or functionality. | 1.4.4 |
| No time limits | There is no time limit on completing a survey. | 2.2.1 |
| No auto-playing media | Nothing plays or animates automatically for more than five seconds. The survey's opening animation is shorter than five seconds and then stops. | 2.2.2 |
| Error identification | When an answer is rejected, the error is identified and described to the respondent in text. | 3.3.1 |
| Error suggestion | Where we know how an input should be corrected, the survey suggests the correction. | 3.3.3 |
| Labels and instructions | Every input field has a descriptive label. | 3.3.2 |
| Review and correction | A **Back** button is shown by default, so respondents can return to an earlier question, review their answer, and change it before submitting. | 3.3.4 |
| Not relying on colour alone | Instructions are written in plain language, and error messages carry both a visual cue and a text cue. | 1.4.1 |

<Note>
  Website and in-app surveys have an optional **auto-close** setting that dismisses an unanswered survey
  prompt after a set number of seconds. It applies only to the first screen, and the countdown is cancelled as
  soon as the respondent interacts with the survey in any way — including with the keyboard. It does not
  impose a time limit on answering, and it does not apply to link surveys.
</Note>

## Known Limitations

The following are known not to conform. Each entry states what a respondent will experience and what a survey
creator can do about it today.

### Embedded Third-Party Video

**Affected criteria:** 1.2.1, 1.2.2, 1.2.3, 1.2.5

Formbricks lets survey creators embed a video from YouTube, Vimeo, or Loom into a question. Formbricks does not
provide any way to upload a transcript, add captions, or supply an audio description or text alternative for
that video. A respondent who is deaf, hard of hearing, blind, or has low vision may not be able to access
information that is only presented in the video.

**Workaround:** Add captions and a transcript to the video on the hosting platform itself, where those features
are available, and repeat the essential information as text in the question headline or description. Do not put
information in a video that a respondent needs in order to answer the question, unless that information also
appears as text.

### Page Titles and Survey Instructions

**Affected criterion:** 2.4.2

Surveys do not currently carry a form title, and survey instructions are not repeated on every page. A
respondent using a screen reader may not be given a clear description of the topic or purpose of the page they
are on.

**Workaround:** Write question headlines that make sense on their own, without depending on a title or on
instructions given earlier in the survey. A fix is planned for an upcoming release.

### Current Language Not Identified in the Language Switcher

**Affected criterion:** 3.1.1

Multi-language surveys show a language switcher. The switcher lists the available languages, but does not
indicate which one is currently active. A respondent cannot tell from the control alone what language the
survey is being presented in.

**Workaround:** None within the survey UI. For link surveys, the `lang` URL parameter can be used to send a
respondent straight to the correct language, so they do not need to use the switcher. See
[Multi-language Surveys](/surveys/general-features/multi-language-surveys). A fix is planned for an upcoming
release.

### Semantic Headings

**Affected criterion:** 2.4.6

The survey UI does not currently use semantic heading elements. A respondent using a screen reader cannot
navigate the survey by heading, and question headlines are not exposed as headings.

**Workaround:** None within the survey UI. Keeping surveys short and putting one question on each screen
reduces the amount of navigation a respondent has to do. A fix is planned for an upcoming release.

### Language of Parts

**Affected criterion:** 3.1.2

Formbricks identifies the survey's language at page level, not per element. A link survey sets the `lang` and
`dir` attributes on the page's `<html>` element from the survey's active language, and updates them when the
respondent uses the language switcher, so assistive technology announces the survey in the right language and
right-to-left languages read in the right direction. No `lang` attribute is set on individual questions,
descriptions, labels, or choice options.

Where a single screen mixes languages — an English question with an untranslated choice option, or a term left
in its original language — the part in the other language is not programmatically identified, and a screen
reader may pronounce it using the wrong language rules.

Website and in-app surveys do not get the page-level tag either. The survey renders inside a page the site
owner controls, whose `lang` describes that page rather than the survey, and Formbricks deliberately leaves it
alone — changing it would misdescribe the rest of the host page. So whenever an embedded survey is shown in a
language other than the host page's, including after the respondent uses the language switcher, its content is
a passage in a different language with no markup saying so.

**Workaround:** Keep every part of a question in one language, and use the multi-language feature to offer the
whole survey in each language rather than mixing languages inside a single question. For website and in-app
surveys, serve the survey in the same language as the page it is embedded in wherever possible.

## Compatibility

The survey UI is a standard web application and runs in current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari,
on desktop and on mobile. It does not require a plugin, an extension, or a separate accessible version.

### How the Assessment Was Carried Out

The most recent evaluation combined automated and manual testing:

- **IBM Equal Access Accessibility Checker** — automated rule-based scanning
- **ANDI (Accessible Name & Description Inspector)** — inspection of accessible names, descriptions, and roles
- **Manual testing** — keyboard-only operation, focus order, and visual inspection

### Assistive Technology

We have **not** formally tested the survey UI against specific named combinations of assistive technology and
browser — for example a particular screen reader paired with a particular browser version. We do not claim
support for combinations we have not evidenced.

If you use a specific assistive technology and hit a barrier in a Formbricks survey, we want to hear about it.
See [Contact](#contact) below.

## Guidance for Survey Creators

Formbricks handles the parts of accessibility that belong to the survey UI. The content you put into a survey is
yours, and it decides whether the finished survey is usable. The following practices make a measurable
difference.

**Write question headlines that stand on their own.** A respondent using a screen reader may hear the headline
without the surrounding context. "How satisfied were you with the onboarding call?" works; "And this one?" does
not.

**Do not put information only in an image.** Formbricks does not currently offer a field for image alt text, so
an image cannot carry information on its own. Treat images as illustration and put anything a respondent needs
in order to answer into the headline or the description.

**Do not convey meaning with colour alone.** If a rating scale, an option, or an instruction depends on a
respondent seeing red versus green, add a label, a number, or a word that carries the same meaning.

**Caption your videos on the hosting platform, and summarise them as text.** See
[Embedded Third-Party Video](#embedded-third-party-video) above.

**Set the survey language correctly.** Choose the right default language and add translations for every
language you send the survey in, so that assistive technology pronounces the content correctly. See
[Multi-language Surveys](/surveys/general-features/multi-language-surveys).

**Leave the Back button enabled.** It is on by default, and it is what allows a respondent to review and correct
an answer before submitting. Turn it off only when the survey design genuinely requires it — for example a quiz
where going back would let a respondent change an answer after seeing the result. See
[Hide Back Button](/surveys/general-features/hide-back-button).

**Keep questions short and ask one thing at a time.** This helps every respondent, and it particularly helps
people using screen readers, screen magnification, or switch access, who move through content more slowly.
