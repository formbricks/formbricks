Dashboards & Charts let you turn Feedback Records into visual analytics. A **Chart** is a single visualization scoped to one Feedback Dataset. A **Dashboard** is a grid of charts you can share with your team.

Charts read Feedback Records, not survey responses. A workspace therefore needs a **Feedback Dataset** and at least
one **Feedback Source** before any chart has data - see the guide below for that path end to end.

## Charts

Charts live under **Analyze → Analysis**. Each chart is a query plus a visualization config.

### Available chart types

| Type | When to use |
| --- | --- |
| **Area Chart** | Trend over time with magnitude emphasis (cumulative volume, NPS trend with shaded area). Default. |
| **Bar Chart** | Compare a measure across categories (responses per source, NPS by segment). |
| **Line Chart** | Trend over time without area fill (CSAT week-over-week, response rate). |
| **Pie Chart** | Share-of-total across a small set of categories (channel mix, sentiment split). |
| **Big Number** | Headline KPI for a dashboard (total responses, avg NPS, % positive). |
| **Matrix** | A grid of two groupings × one measure. Built for matrix (Likert) questions: statements as rows, scale points as columns, each cell the share of that statement's answers. Works for any two groupings too (country × source). |

You can build a chart in two ways:

### Manual builder

Pick a Feedback Dataset, choose dimensions and measures (count of records, average NPS, ...), apply filters, and select a chart type. Live preview updates as you tweak.

### Filter by embedded data

Embedded data that a Feedback Source carries on its records (a plan, a region, a customer segment) shows up in
the builder under **Embedded data**, next to the dataset's other fields. You can group by an embedded-data field and
filter on it like any other text field. The builder lists the 100 most frequent embedded-data keys of the dataset;
a chart already saved on a rarer key keeps working.

### Filter by answers to another question

Use **Answer to question** in the filters to keep only the records whose submission answered another question of the
dataset a certain way, for example "comments from people who gave an NPS of 9 or 10" or "CSAT of customers who chose
*Enterprise* as their plan". Pick the question, then the answer it must have; for single-choice and rating questions
you can also pick an answer it must not have. A multiple-choice question with several selections matches when any
of the chosen options was selected, and has no "is not". A submission that skipped
the question never matches.

A chart takes up to 5 answer filters, and you can combine them with other filters in AND and OR groups.

### Matrix chart

To chart a matrix question:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick the Matrix type">
    In the chart builder, select **Matrix** in the chart type switch above the preview.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick your matrix question">
    Choose the question under **Matrix question** at the top of the left panel. Formbricks fills in the rows (the statements), the columns (the scale points) and the measure (**Count**) for you, in the order they appear in your survey.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Adjust how it reads">
    Under the preview, choose what each cell shows (**Percent**, **Count** or **Both**), and turn the **Color scale**, **Totals** and **Swap rows and columns** on or off.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Percentages are always taken per statement, so each statement adds up to 100%. Hover a cell to see its count and the number of answers behind it.

To build any other matrix, turn on **Group data** and pick two fields — the first becomes the rows, the second the columns — plus exactly one measure. A matrix shows up to 30 rows and 15 columns; add a filter to narrow a larger one down.

### AI builder

Describe what you want in natural language ("Average NPS by month over the last 90 days") and Formbricks generates the Cube query for you. You can edit the result in the manual builder afterwards.

The AI builder requires **Smart functionality (AI)** to be enabled at the organization level and a configured AI provider. See [AI Features](/platform/features/ai-features).

## Dashboards

Dashboards live under **Analyze → Analysis**, alongside charts. Each dashboard is a grid you can resize and arrange.

From a dashboard you can:

- **Create** a new chart inline and have it added automatically.
- **Add existing** charts.
- **Reorder and resize** widgets.
- **Duplicate** a chart for a quick variation; the copy lands on the same dashboard.
- **Delete** a widget (the underlying chart stays in the workspace).

A chart can sit on several dashboards, and editing it changes it on all of them. To change it on one dashboard
only, duplicate it there and remove the original from that dashboard. A chart that is still on a dashboard can't be
deleted: the delete dialog lists the dashboards that hold it, so you can remove it from them first.

If someone else saves the dashboard while you are editing it, saving tells you so. Choose **Reload** to see their
version and drop your changes, or **Save mine anyway** to keep yours; the dialog names any charts they added that
saving yours would remove.

## Dashboard filters

Dashboard filters narrow all of a dashboard's charts at once, for example to one region, one product, or one plan.
They use the same **Filter** button, **Advanced filter** and filter chips as the responses page, and sit at the top
of the dashboard next to the date range.

### Add your own filters

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Filter menu">
    Open the dashboard and click the **Filter** button. Fields with a few fixed values, such as sentiment, value
    band or field type, can be ticked right there.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a rule in Advanced filter">
    Choose **Advanced filter**, pick a field, and the operator for it is chosen for you; change it if you need
    (**is**, **is not**, **contains**, **is greater than**, **is after**, ...). Then set the value: pick it from a
    list, type it, or choose a day, depending on the field.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**. Each rule becomes a chip under the bar and narrows every chart right away.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A filter can use any field the charts can be grouped by, including embedded-data fields. Answer filters are not
offered here: they belong in each chart, in the chart builder.

### Values to pick from

- **Fields whose values are looked up**, such as the source name, the field label, or an embedded-data field, list
  the values that occur in the dashboard's charts for **is** and **is not**, narrowed by the date range and the other
  filters. Search the list to find more; it shows up to 100 values at a time. For **contains** you type the text.
- **Fields with a fixed set of options**, such as sentiment, value band or field type, always list all of their
  options, whatever the charts hold.
- **Free-text fields**, such as the response text or the user ID, and number fields take a value you type, and date
  fields a day you pick. Free-text fields never list values, so the text of individual responses isn't shown to
  everyone who opens the dashboard.

Use the date range to pick the period every chart shows. It narrows the period each chart was saved with; on a
chart grouped by time, **All time** keeps the chart's own period.

Your filters and date range are your own: they are remembered for this dashboard in your browser, and they don't
change what anyone else sees. They are also part of the page address, so a link you share opens the dashboard with
the same filters.

### Lock filters for everyone

An owner, manager or member who can edit the dashboard can lock filters for everyone who opens it:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set the filters">
    Add the filters you want, as above.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lock them">
    Click **Lock for everyone**. The filters are saved on the dashboard and show with a lock, before each person's own.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A locked filter applies to every reader, Dashboard viewers included, and they can't remove it; their own filters
narrow further. To unlock a filter, an editor clicks the **×** on its chip. A dashboard holds up to 10 locked filters.
The date range is never locked.

### Filters only narrow a chart

A dashboard filter is added on top of each chart's own filters and never replaces them. A chart saved for
products A and B shows nothing when you filter for product C, rather than showing product C. A value list only offers
values that at least one chart on the dashboard can show.

If a chart's Feedback Dataset doesn't have the field a filter uses, the chart can't be narrowed by it. The chart then
says "This chart can't be filtered by" and names the filter, instead of showing data the filter would have left out.

<Note>
  A filter's value list shows the values of that field to everyone who can open the dashboard, Dashboard viewers
  included. Before you filter on an embedded-data field such as an email address or a name, consider whether
  everyone with access should see those values.
</Note>

Charts and dashboards are also available through the [MCP server](/platform/mcp/overview), so an agent can build
them for you.

## Permissions

- Owners, Managers, and members with **Manage** or **Read & Write** access can create and edit dashboards and charts.
- Members with **Read** access can view dashboards and charts but cannot edit them.
- Dashboard viewers can open the dashboards of the Workspaces their teams have access to and use their filters, and
  see nothing else. See [Dashboard viewer](/platform/features/user-management/organizations-and-roles#dashboard-viewer-role).
- Everyone who can open a dashboard can add their own filters, which only they see; only those who can edit it can
  lock filters for everyone or unlock them.

## Requirements

- A Feedback Dataset linked to your workspace.
- At least one Feedback Source writing into it - a dataset with no source stays empty.
- Workspace access to that dataset.

If Analysis shows **"No feedback dataset linked"**, start with
[Creating your first charts](/unify-feedback/dashboards-charts/creating-your-first-charts).
