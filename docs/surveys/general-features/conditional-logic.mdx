Conditional logic is a set of rules attached to a **block**. Each rule reads *when this is true, then do that* — skip ahead, require an answer, or update a variable.

## Where logic lives

Logic belongs to the block, not to a single question. You add and edit it on the [Flow tab](/surveys/general-features/flow-tab), next to Questions in the survey editor. The Flow tab lists every block with its rules indented under it, so you see the whole survey flow in one place. Click a rule there to open the editor described below.

## Terminology

* **Condition**: a rule that decides whether an action runs.

* **Action**: what happens when the condition is met.

## Building a rule

On the Flow tab, click `Add logic +` under a block, choose **Jump to block** or **Set value**, and fill in the sentence. A Set value step always runs until you give it a condition with **Add a condition**.

## Actions

* **Set value**: change a variable's value. The result is available to every later question, and to the rules below this one.

* **Require answer**: make an optional question required. Only questions that are optional to begin with can be required this way.

* **Jump to block**: send the respondent to a specific block instead of the next one.

* **Mark response as Low quality**: mark the response Low quality when its answers are implausible, for example *If “hours” is greater than 112 → Mark response as Low quality*. See [Mark as low quality](#mark-as-low-quality).

<Note>
  A rule can carry several actions. They run in the order they are listed.
</Note>

## Mark as low quality

Some answers show that a response can't be trusted: someone who streams more than 112 hours a week, or who rates a product they said they never used. A rule with the **Mark response as Low quality** action marks such a response for you, so nobody has to review every response by hand.

* **The respondent sees nothing.** The survey carries on as it would without the rule, and the rule's other actions, such as a jump, still run.
* **The response is marked like one a reviewer marked.** It shows as Low quality in your responses, it is left out of the [Analysis export](/surveys/analysis/export-responses) by default, and it is removed from Dashboards.
* **You can restore it.** A response a rule marked is restored like any other Low quality response.
* **A rule can only mark.** Nothing a respondent does later clears the mark, and a rule never changes a response a reviewer already marked.

The action is available in a block's rules and in the [rules before the first question](/surveys/general-features/flow-tab#rules-before-the-first-question). There it reads URL parameters and hidden fields, for example *If “source” equals “panel-x” → Mark response as Low quality* for a panel source you don't trust: the respondent still answers the whole survey, and the response is marked as soon as it is saved. To keep such respondents out instead, jump to an ending there to screen them out.

## Everyone else

Under the rules sits **All other answers will continue to**. This is the fallback for respondents who match none of the conditions, and it is set to the next block unless you change it.

## Several conditions in one rule

The `⋮` button beside a condition adds another condition below it, duplicates it, removes it, or wraps it in a **group**. Conditions are joined with `and` or `or`, and a group is how you mix the two without ambiguity — the same reason brackets exist in arithmetic. Removing the last condition of a rule that jumps or requires an answer removes the rule; a rule that only sets values or marks the response Low quality stays and reads **Always**.

## Several rules in one block

`Add logic +` adds another rule. Rules are evaluated top to bottom, and the first one whose condition holds decides where the respondent goes. Open a rule on the Flow tab and use **Move up** or **Move down** to change the order.
