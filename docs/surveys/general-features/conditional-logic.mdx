Conditional logic is a set of rules attached to a **block**. Each rule reads *when this is true, then do that* — skip ahead, require an answer, or update a variable.

## Where logic lives

Logic belongs to the block, not to a single question. You add and edit it on the [Flow tab](/surveys/general-features/flow-tab), next to Questions in the survey editor. The Flow tab lists every block with its rules indented under it, so you see the whole survey flow in one place. Click a rule there to open the editor described below.

## Terminology

* **Condition**: a rule that decides whether an action runs.

* **Action**: what happens when the condition is met.

## Building a rule

On the Flow tab, click `Add logic +` under a block, choose **Jump rule** or **Calculation**, and fill in the sentence.

![A rule that routes savings-account respondents to a second block](/images/surveys/general-features/conditional-logic/logic-rule.webp)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose what to test">
    The **When** row starts with a source. It can be the answer to a **question**, the value of a **variable**, or the value of a **hidden field**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose an operator">
    The operators on offer depend on the source. A single-select question can be compared with `Equals`, `Does not equal`, `Equals one of` and `Is submitted`; a free text question adds `Contains`, `Starts with`, `Ends with` and their negations.

    ![The operator list for a single-select question](/images/surveys/general-features/conditional-logic/condition-operators.webp)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose a value">
    Compare against a fixed value, or against another question's answer, a variable, or a hidden field.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose an action">
    The **Then** row is what happens when the condition holds.

    ![The three actions: Calculate, Require Answer and Jump to block](/images/surveys/general-features/conditional-logic/action-options.webp)
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Actions

* **Calculate**: change a variable's value. The result is available to every later question, and to the rules below this one.

* **Require Answer**: make an optional question required. Only questions that are optional to begin with can be required this way.

* **Jump to block**: send the respondent to a specific block instead of the next one.

* **Mark as low quality**: mark the response Low quality when its answers are implausible, for example *If “hours” is greater than 112 → Mark as low quality*. See [Mark as low quality](#mark-as-low-quality).

<Note>
  A rule can carry several actions. They run in the order they are listed.
</Note>

## Mark as low quality

Some answers show that a response can't be trusted: someone who streams more than 112 hours a week, or who rates a product they said they never used. A rule with the **Mark as low quality** action marks such a response for you, so nobody has to review every response by hand.

* **The respondent sees nothing.** The survey carries on as it would without the rule, and the rule's other actions, such as a jump, still run.
* **The response is marked like one a reviewer marked.** It shows as Low quality in your responses, it is left out of the [Analysis export](/surveys/analysis/export-responses) by default, and it is removed from Dashboards.
* **You can restore it.** A response a rule marked is restored like any other Low quality response.
* **A rule can only mark.** Nothing a respondent does later clears the mark, and a rule never changes a response a reviewer already marked.

The action is available in a block's rules. It isn't offered in the rules before the first question, because nothing has been answered yet; use a jump to an ending there to screen respondents out.

## Everyone else

Under the rules sits **All other answers will continue to**. This is the fallback for respondents who match none of the conditions, and it is set to the next block unless you change it.

## Several conditions in one rule

The `⋮` button beside a condition adds another condition below it, duplicates it, removes it, or wraps it in a **group**. Conditions are joined with `and` or `or`, and a group is how you mix the two without ambiguity — the same reason brackets exist in arithmetic.

## Several rules in one block

`Add logic +` adds another rule. Rules are evaluated top to bottom, and the first one whose condition holds decides where the respondent goes. Open a rule on the Flow tab and use **Move up** or **Move down** to change the order.
