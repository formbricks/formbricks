The Feedback Box gives your users a direct channel to share what is on their mind, in two clicks, without leaving your app.

## Purpose

A low-friction channel catches the small frustrations that never make it into a support ticket. Because it is always available rather than scheduled, it also tells you *when* something broke.

## What you will build

A survey that opens whenever someone clicks your Feedback button, and that keeps working every time they click it — not once per person.

<Note>
  This is an app survey, so it needs the Formbricks widget running in your product. If you have not
  installed it yet, follow [the quickstart](/surveys/website-app-surveys/quickstart) first.
</Note>

## 1. Start from the template

Open **Surveys**, click **New Survey** and choose **Choose a template**. Search for **Feedback Box**.

![The Feedback Box template in the template picker](/images/surveys/best-practices/feedback-box/template.webp)

Hovering a template previews it on the right. The Feedback Box template opens with a "What is on your mind?" choice and branches into a follow-up question for each answer.

## 2. Make it yours

Edit the questions on the **Questions** tab. The wording matters more than it looks: "What is on your mind, boss?" sets a casual tone that suits some products and not others.

Keep it short. Every extra question costs you responses, and the value of a feedback box is that answering it is nearly free.

## 3. Trigger it on your Feedback button

Open the **Settings** tab and find **Survey Trigger**. Click **Add action**, then **Capture new action**.

![The Add action dialog, on its Capture new action tab](/images/surveys/best-practices/feedback-box/action.webp)

Name the action, leave the type on **Click**, and identify your button in one of two ways:

- **Inner Text** — matches the visible text of the element that was clicked. Switch it on and enter `Feedback` if that is what your button says.
- **CSS Selector** — matches a class or id, such as `#feedback-button`. Use this when the label is translated, or when several elements share the text.

Switch on both and the click has to match both to count.

Once created, the action appears under the trigger:

![The Survey Trigger section with the Feedback action attached](/images/surveys/best-practices/feedback-box/trigger.webp)

<Tip>
  Reusing a button that already exists? **Select saved action** lists every action in the workspace, so
  you do not have to define the same click twice.
</Tip>

## 4. Let it open every time

By default a survey is shown to each person once. That is wrong for a feedback box — someone who reports a bug on Monday should still be able to request a feature on Tuesday.

Still on **Settings**, open **Visibility & Recontact** and choose **Keep showing while conditions match**.

![Recontact options, set to keep showing while conditions match](/images/surveys/best-practices/feedback-box/recontact.webp)

<Note>
  The workspace-wide [Cooldown Period](/surveys/website-app-surveys/cooldown-period) still applies on top
  of this. If your instance is set to one survey per week, that gate runs first. **Ignore Cooldown
  Period**, in the same section, exempts this survey — which is usually what you want for a box the user
  opened deliberately.
</Note>

## 5. Publish

**Publish** puts it live. The widget picks up the change within a minute.

## Where the feedback goes

Responses land in the survey's **Responses** tab like any other. Two things worth setting up on day one:

- A [Workflow](/workflows/overview) that emails whoever triages feedback, so nobody has to remember to check.
- [Tags](/surveys/general-features/tags) on the responses, so "bug" and "feature request" stay separable once there are hundreds of them.
